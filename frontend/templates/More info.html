System Architecture

A multimodal deep learning pipeline fusing CT imaging, clinical parameters, and genomic data for comprehensive lung cancer prognosis.

End-to-end pipeline

Step 01

CT scan upload

PNG, JPG, or DICOM image accepted and stored server-side

Step 02

CT inference

ResNet-50 binary + 4-class type classification with softmax

Step 03

Grad-CAM

Gradient-weighted attention heatmap for visual explainability

Step 04

Clinical scoring

Age bracket, disease stage, and smoking risk computation

Step 05

Genomic data

Optional microarray / expression CSV for additional weight

Step 06

Fusion output

Weighted ensemble → risk tier, survival %, and confidence

Fusion weight distribution

60%
CT imaging
Dominant signal — cancer probability from the binary model
30%
Clinical data
Age bracket, cancer stage (I–IV), and smoking status
10%
Genomic data
Optional — adds a penalty when a CSV is provided
Fusion formula fusion_score   = (0.6 × ct_cancer_prob) + (0.3 × clinical_score) + (0.1 × genomic_score)
survival      = 1.0 − fusion_score
confidence    = (type_conf × 0.7 + cancer_prob × 0.3) ^ 0.75

Clinical risk scoring

Parameter Value Score Rationale
Age ≥ 65 years +0.30 Highest age-related risk bracket
50 – 64 years +0.20 Intermediate risk bracket
< 50 years +0.10 Lower age-related risk
Stage I Early localised +0.10 Confined to lung — best prognosis
Stage II Regional spread +0.30 Limited nodal involvement
Stage III Extensive regional +0.60 Advanced locoregional disease
Stage IV Distant metastasis +0.90 Metastatic — poorest prognosis
Smoking Current / Former +0.30 Primary lung cancer risk factor
Smoking Never +0.00 No smoking penalty applied

All scores are clamped to [0.05, 0.95] before fusion to prevent extreme edge outputs.

Risk level thresholds

Low risk

0 – 30%
Routine follow-up and healthy lifestyle advised. Likely normal scan or very early stage.

Medium risk

30 – 60%
Enhanced monitoring and specialist referral recommended. Standard-plus care applies.

High risk

60 – 100%
Immediate oncologist consultation, biopsy, and PET/CT staging strongly advised.

Detectable cancer subtypes

Class Subtype Prevalence Key notes
Class 0 Adenocarcinoma ~40% of NSCLC Most common; peripheral; targetable EGFR / ALK mutations frequent
Class 1 Large cell carcinoma ~10–15% of NSCLC Undifferentiated; fast-growing; diagnosed by exclusion
Class 2 Normal — No malignant findings; routine screening interval recommended
Class 3 Squamous cell carcinoma ~25–30% of NSCLC Centrally located; strongly associated with smoking history
